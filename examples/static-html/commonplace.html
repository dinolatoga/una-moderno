In praise of the commonplace book

An unremarkable notebook for thoughts that are not ready to become anything else.

By Mara SolisOctober 6, 2026Making things

In this article

Collecting without a system

The notebook in this fictional essay begins with a shopping list and ends, so far, with the name of a tree. In between are overheard phrases, book titles, a recipe adjustment, and an idea that made sense on a bus.

A commonplace book does not ask for a finished argument. It offers a place for fragments to live near one another. Its usefulness comes partly from the accidents of that proximity.

Returning to a page

I leave a few blank pages at the back for an index. Most months I forget to update it. The book remains useful anyway, because looking for one thing often turns up another.

The aim is not to capture everything. It is to keep a few things long enough to discover what they might mean together.

Take your time.
